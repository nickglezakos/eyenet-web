The bet

EyeNet’s differentiators are the de facto optimal penetrators of the TAM: any camera as a vision node, on-prem detect → decide → act, no per-camera license tax, first value in minutes, hardware-bound scale.

That is how you enter the market where incumbents can only rent cloud storage on locked camera hardware and complicated server setups — and expand room-to-campus without a license ceiling.

Product Cloud storage, bandwidth, recordings Sovereignty + operational action
Video Cloud by default On-prem by default — never has to leave the site
Cameras Passive recorders / “AI boxes” Vision nodes on a ~16 GB PC — any IP / Wi‑Fi / phone camera
Licensing Per-camera licenses No per-camera licenses

How we size: Dollar TAM bands are EyeNet’s conservative U.S. addressable ranges, grounded in third-party category reports [9]–[12]. B2B / Enterprise $2–4B sits inside U.S. video analytics (MarketsandMarkets: ~$2.3B in 2023 → ~$5.7B by 2028 [10]; Grand View: global $12.7B in 2024 with North America ~⅓ [9]). B2C $0.5–1.5B is a software / operational-action slice of the larger U.S. smart-home security camera market (~$3.7B in 2024 [11]), with U.S. VSaaS (~$1.3B [12]) as the cloud-incumbent adjacent. Share → revenue is plan arithmetic on those bands (1–3% of B2C; 1–2% of enterprise). Bottom-up GTM pocket counts: How it is sold and Reference [1]–[8].
