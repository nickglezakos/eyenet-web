01

What is EyeNet

EyeNet is on-premises computer vision that turns ordinary cameras into vision nodes — they detect, decide, and act on site. Video stays local by default. One platform serves homes, care, offices, shops, security posts, ships, and plants.

What EyeNet does

The platform selling points that cut across every persona — why EyeNet is not another camera cloud.

EyeNet runs on any camera — existing cams, phones, PCs, or inexpensive ordinary cameras, with first detection in minutes on a ~16 GB PC. Not vendor lock-in.

EyeNet detects, decides, and acts — targeted alerts to mobile, email, webhooks, signage, gates, and the ERP/CRM, only when something needs a person. Not passive VMS.

EyeNet stays on-prem by default — processing and footage stay on premises, with full operation when the internet is down, and essential updates still reach HQ even on a weak or limited connection.

EyeNet scales without a per-camera tax — scale is the mesh you deploy, from one room to a campus, not a license ceiling or a GPU farm.

EyeNet starts with zero / near-zero hardware — staff use their own PC or mobile for presence grants; sites reuse cameras they already own, including thousands of employees without a camera fleet project.

EyeNet serves six wedges on one fabric — HR presence, adaptive signage, home and away, Virtual Ward, night / perimeter security, shipping and industrial on Presence, Vradar, Sentinel, and Matrix.

Who buys — and why now

Real buyers, real jobs: people today hit a tech wall with incumbents — with EyeNet they can start on moderate hardware they choose or already have and get the outcome today.

An HR manager can now let every employee check in from their own PC or phone — and push presence grants to the ERP/CRM — with zero new hardware, even at thousands of seats.

A shop or mall owner can now run intelligent signage end to end — prove who is watching each screen with full audience analytics, load and switch playlists, and let marketers log in to manage the full campaign lifecycle — on cams they already have, without a locked player kit.

A parent can now turn a door cam into an intelligent doorbell — alerts for known and unknown people, plus webhooks to smart-home screens and sound — on cams they already own or a cheap everyday camera, with no cloud subscription. Default is alerts only with footage kept local; a live view is available only when everyone involved consents.

A parent of a student living away can now watch a remote flat or cottage with one ordinary cam — phone pings only, no guard contract or cloud VMS. Stakeholders get the ping without shipping video off-site, and can open a live stream only if all parties agree.

A care provider can now turn a patient room cam into fall and help-call events for the clinical stack — with alerts to all stakeholders, caregivers and next of kin, escalated per settings — without shipping cloud video. Next of kin stay assured without being glued to a camera: they get instant alerts for their loved ones, full awareness of who is on premises, and whether caregivers are there and on time, with a live feed available at any time.

A security ops lead can now get night vigilance and perimeter from the fence and floor cams already there — without per-camera seats or another night shift.

A captain or plant ops lead can now know who’s onboard and who’s in zone when the link dies — on the cams already aboard or on site. HQs get real-time alerts per settings plus full insights and analytics, scalable from one site to entire ships, fleets, or industrial areas, with recordings and live feed available.

What changes

Users are not locked to a camera wall. The platform watches 24/7; the right person gets a targeted alert — mobile, email, webhook, clinical app, or HubSpot — only when something needs them. Ordinary cameras become an always-on assistant that decides and acts on premises, including when the internet is down.

Edge Not cloud-bound

On-device AI. Full offline. Data sovereignty by default. Processing and storage stay on premises.

Action Not passive VMS

Triggers fire mobile, email, webhooks, signage, and gates. Each camera has its own rules.

Scale Hardware-bound

Any IP, WiFi, or phone camera. ~16 GB PC. One room to an enterprise mesh. No GPU farm.

Incumbents sell

  • Cloud storage & monthly bandwidth
  • Passive recordings & dashboards
  • Generic “AI boxes”
  • Projects that take quarters

EyeNet ships

  • On-prem decisions & zero video cloud
  • Targeted notifications that run the operation
  • Purpose-built apps for real jobs
  • Detection in minutes — scale by hardware

How it multiplies operator capacity

  • Vision nodes — face, count, demographics, and routes on cameras the site already owns.
  • Entrances, restricted zones, VIP watchlists — headcount, unauthorised presence, and person-of-interest alerts without extra posts.
  • Signage that reads the room — age, gender, and group size switch content in milliseconds, with impressions and dwell.
  • Remote control from a phone — VPN access to cameras, triggers, and dashboards. Footage stays on premises even when the operator does not.
