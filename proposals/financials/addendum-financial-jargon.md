# Addendum — Financial Jargon in Plain English

**Who this is for.** Anyone reading the EyeNet financial studies or pitch deck who is not a finance specialist.  
**Companions.** `use-of-proceeds-study.md` · `pricing-and-revenue-estimations.md` · `financial-prerequisites.md` · `EyeNet Financials.pptx`

## 1. One-page cheat sheet

| Term | Plain English |
|------|----------------|
| **Revenue** | Money customers pay you |
| **ARR** | Yearly software revenue run-rate (if today’s subscriptions kept going for a year) |
| **MRR** | Same idea, per month |
| **ACV** | Typical yearly value of one business customer contract |
| **CAC** | What you spend to win one paying customer |
| **LTV** | Total money you expect from one customer over their life with you |
| **LTV:CAC** | “How many dollars back per dollar spent to acquire” (e.g. 30:1) |
| **Payback** | Months until that customer’s payments cover their CAC |
| **Retention** | Share of customers who stay (85–90% = most stay) |
| **Churn** | Share who leave (opposite of retention) |
| **TAM** | Biggest market size you could theoretically sell into |
| **Seed** | Early investment round to launch and prove sales |
| **Burn floor** | Minimum cash needed to operate for about a year |
| **Use of proceeds** | How invested money will be spent |
| **FTE** | One full-time person’s worth of work |
| **Pipeline** | List of potential deals being worked |
| **Logo** | One customer account (a “name on the customer list”) |
| **Breakeven** | Point where the business stops losing money overall |
| **Series A** | Next larger funding round after seed, usually to scale sales |
| **SAFE / note** | Simple early investment papers (investor puts in cash now; equity terms later) |
| **Pre-money / post-money** | Company value *before* / *after* the new cash is added |
| **Dilution** | Founders’ % ownership shrinks when new investors buy in |
| **Unit economics** | Whether *one* customer is profitable on paper (CAC vs LTV, payback) |
| **Land-and-expand** | Start small with a customer, then sell them more later |
| **Freemium** | Free trial or free tier, then upsell to paid |
| **GTM** | Go-to-market — how you sell and market |
| **Underwrite** | An investor’s judgment: “Can I trust these numbers?” |

## 2. Money coming in (revenue & pricing)

### Revenue
**What it means.** Cash customers pay for the product.  
**EyeNet example.** Plan path ~$1M (2027) → ~$8M (2028) → ~$26M (2029).

### ARR — Annual Recurring Revenue
**What it means.** “If we keep today’s subscriptions for a full year, how much is that?” It is a **run-rate**, not always cash already in the bank.  
**EyeNet example.** Seed proof asks for **$50–100K ARR** — a small but real subscription base before scaling.

### MRR — Monthly Recurring Revenue
**What it means.** Same idea as ARR, counted per month. Roughly ARR ≈ MRR × 12.  
**EyeNet example.** B2C modeled average **~$50 MRR** per paying consumer.

### ACV — Annual Contract Value
**What it means.** How much one **business** customer is worth per year on their contract.  
**EyeNet example.** B2B path **~$12–36K** per account; plan mix implies average ACV rising **~$13K → $22K → $28K**.

### Blended ACV
**What it means.** Average across a mix of cheap and expensive contracts (Starter + Pro + Enterprise), not one price tag.  
**Why it matters.** Hitting “50 customers” is not enough if each pays too little.

### Package / tier / SKU
**What it means.** Named product offering with a price (e.g. Starter, Professional, Lite).  
**EyeNet example.** B2C Lite SKUs (Signage Lite, Home Security Lite, etc.).

### Freemium
**What it means.** Let people use something free (or free for a while), then ask them to pay.  
**EyeNet example.** Free funnel **60–90 days**, then Lite / Pro.

### Self-serve
**What it means.** Customer buys online with a card — no salesperson required.  
**Opposite.** Sales-assisted (a person helps close the deal).

### Premium / uplift (e.g. +40–60%)
**What it means.** Extra charge for a richer version (here: mobile / wearable variants).

### Contract band
**What it means.** The allowed price range for deals (from low to high).  
**EyeNet example.** Enterprise contracts **$6K–$150K+/year**.

## 3. Cost of getting customers (growth math)

### CAC — Customer Acquisition Cost
**What it means.** Average marketing + sales spend to win **one** paying customer.  
**EyeNet example.** B2C **$20–30**; Enterprise **$3–5K**. Business customers cost far more to win than consumers.

### LTV — Lifetime Value
**What it means.** Total revenue expected from one customer before they leave.  
**EyeNet example.** Enterprise modeled LTV **~$144K**.

### LTV:CAC (ratio)
**What it means.** How many dollars of customer lifetime value you get for each dollar spent to acquire them.  
**EyeNet example.** B2C ~**30:1**, Enterprise ~**40:1** — plan models, not yet proven by real cohorts.

### Payback (period)
**What it means.** How many months until that customer’s payments repay what you spent to win them.  
**EyeNet example.** B2C **1–2 months**; B2B **~3–4 months**. Shorter is healthier.

### Unit economics
**What it means.** Looking at **one customer** as a mini P&L: Does winning them make sense?  
**EyeNet rule.** Seed check size does **not** change unit economics — bigger seed only buys **speed**.

### Cohort / cohort-backed
**What it means.** Real groups of customers tracked over time.  
**Plain warning.** “Not yet cohort-backed” = these CAC/LTV numbers are **plans**, not history.

### Conversion
**What it means.** Turning a free user or lead into a **paying** customer.

### Pipeline / lead
**What it means.** A **lead** is a possible buyer; the **pipeline** is the list of leads/deals in progress.  
**EyeNet example.** Seed asks for a **50-lead pipeline**.

### Logo
**What it means.** One named customer account. “50 logos” = 50 customer companies (or accounts).

### Land-and-expand
**What it means.** Win a small first deal (“land”), then sell more sites, apps, or seats later (“expand”).  
**Why it matters.** That is how blended ACV can rise from ~$13K toward ~$28K.

### Retention
**What it means.** Percent of customers who stay over a period.  
**EyeNet example.** B2B **85–90%** retention → only **10–15%** leave each year.

### Churn
**What it means.** Percent who cancel or do not renew. High churn forces you to replace lost customers before you can grow.

### Net new (logos)
**What it means.** New customers minus those who left. Ending count = start + net new.

## 4. Market size words

### TAM — Total Addressable Market
**What it means.** The full dollar size of the market you *could* sell into if you won everything relevant.  
**EyeNet example.** B2C **$0.5–1.5B**; B2B/Enterprise **$2–4B** (U.S. plan bands).

### Target share
**What it means.** The slice of TAM you aim to capture (e.g. 1–2%).  
**EyeNet example.** 1–2% of B2B/Enterprise TAM → **~$20–80M** opportunity (long-term ceiling context, not next year’s P&L).

### Pocket / addressable pocket
**What it means.** A countable group of possible buyers (e.g. ~99K public K–12 schools).

### Motion
**What it means.** A distinct way of selling (consumer self-serve vs mid-market sales vs enterprise).  
**EyeNet.** Three motions: **B2C Light**, **B2B Starter/Pro**, **Enterprise**.

### B2C / B2B
**B2C** = selling to consumers / small buyers. **B2B** = selling to businesses.

## 5. Funding & ownership words

### Seed (round)
**What it means.** Early money to launch the product and prove first sales.  
**EyeNet ladder.** **$500K** burn · **$1M** base · **$2.5M** accelerate — choices on one ladder, not three different businesses.

### Burn / burn floor
**What it means.** Cash the company spends to operate. **Burn floor** = minimum needed for about a year of lean operations.  
**EyeNet.** **$500K** burn floor.

### Runway
**What it means.** How many months you can keep operating with cash on hand before you need more money or profits.

### Use of proceeds
**What it means.** The spending plan for invested money (people, marketing, tech, offices, etc.).

### Speed capital
**What it means.** Extra money above the burn floor used to move **faster** (more ads, more salespeople, more offices) — not to invent a new business model.

### Series A
**What it means.** The next major round after seed, usually to scale a sales machine.  
**EyeNet.** Size depends on which seed you chose (~$0.7–1M after burn; ~$2–3M on the base path).

### SAFE / convertible note
**What it means.** Investor gives cash **now**; exact ownership % is set later (often at the next priced round). Common for early seed.

### Pre-money valuation
**What it means.** What the company is said to be worth **before** new investment cash is added.  
**EyeNet base seed.** ~**$3M pre**.

### Post-money valuation
**What it means.** Pre-money + new cash in.  
**EyeNet base.** ~**$4M post** ($3M + $1M).

### Dilution
**What it means.** Existing owners own a **smaller percentage** after new investors buy shares. The pie gets shared with more people.

### Equity / ownership
**What it means.** Percentage of the company you own.

### Instrument
**What it means.** The legal form of the investment (SAFE, note, preferred stock, etc.).

### Underwrite / underwritable
**What it means.** Investor language for “I can defend believing this plan.” An underwritable path has clear proof points and sensible unit economics.

## 6. Team & spending words

### FTE — Full-Time Equivalent
**What it means.** One full-time person’s year of work. **0.5 FTE** = half-time. **4.5 FTE** = four and a half full-time roles.  
**Note.** EyeNet `invest` docs do not publish headcount; FTE tables in the study are **derived** from dollar budgets ÷ assumed salaries.

### Fully-loaded cost
**What it means.** Salary **plus** typical extras (taxes, benefits, tools) — the true yearly cost of employing someone.

### Personnel
**What it means.** Money spent on people (wages / contractor labor in the plan).

### OpEx — Operating expenses
**What it means.** Day-to-day costs of running the company (people, marketing, tools, rent), not buying a factory.

### Non-people / program spend
**What it means.** Money that is **not** salaries — ads, events, cloud, legal, office setup.

### GTM — Go-to-market
**What it means.** The whole system of marketing, sales, partners, and packaging used to win customers.

### AE / SE / SDR (sales roles)
**Plain English.**  
- **AE** (Account Executive) — closes deals.  
- **SE** (Sales Engineer / Solutions) — technical help in sales.  
- **SDR** (Sales Development) — books meetings / qualifies leads.  
**Partner** — another company that resells or introduces you.

### CS — Customer Success
**What it means.** Team that helps customers adopt the product and renew (protects retention).

### Footprint
**What it means.** Where the company is physically / legally set up (e.g. Greece, U.S., London representation).

### Pilot
**What it means.** A first real-world trial with a customer — often discounted or limited — to prove the product works before a full contract.

### Channel partner
**What it means.** A distributor / reseller / introducer who helps find and sell customers.  
**EyeNet seed ask.** **2–3 partners** live.

## 7. Health of the business

### Breakeven
**What it means.** Income covers costs — the company is no longer losing money overall.  
**EyeNet plan.** Around **2028** (~$8M revenue) as the breakeven year in the path.

### Gross margin
**What it means.** After the direct cost of delivering the product, what % of revenue is left (before office/sales overhead). Higher is usually better for software.

### Profitability / “solidly profitable”
**What it means.** Consistently making more than you spend (beyond just breakeven).  
**EyeNet plan language.** **2029** on the three motions.

### Exit
**What it means.** Owners cash out — typically IPO (public listing) or sale to a larger company.  
**EyeNet target range.** **$500M–$1B** valuation in **2030–2031** (plan target, not a promise).

### Valuation
**What it means.** What the company is priced at in a funding or sale context.

## 8. Phrases you will see in these EyeNet docs

| Phrase | Plain English |
|--------|----------------|
| **Burn first, then speed** | Fund the minimum year of ops first; only then decide how much extra cash buys faster growth |
| **Unit economics hold across seed scenarios** | $500K, $1M, and $2.5M do not invent different customer math — same CAC/LTV story |
| **Capital buys speed, not a rewrite** | More money → more people/ads/offices sooner, not a new product thesis |
| **Financials hold** | Logo counts, ARR gates, retention, and average deal size stay on the plan path |
| **Derived** | We calculated this from other published numbers; it is not a separate official invest table |
| **Planning rates / planning assumptions** | Helpful assumptions for modeling (e.g. $/FTE); not always printed in `invest` |
| **In-seed proof** | What you must show during the seed period itself (pilots, ARR, pipeline) |
| **Path toward ~200 customers** | Series A story aims at the ~200 B2B stock associated with the ~$8M year |
| **Mix richens** | Average deal gets larger (more Pro/Enterprise), not only more logos |
| **Scarce object** | The hard thing to win (here: paying B2B logos, not cheap B2C clicks) |

## 9. Tiny examples with EyeNet numbers

**ARR vs one contract**  
If one Starter customer pays ~$750/month → ~$9K/year ACV.  
Ten such customers ≈ **~$90K ARR** — inside the seed **$50–100K ARR** gate.

**CAC payback**  
Spend **$25** to win a B2C user at **$50/month** → payback in about **half a month to one month** of subscription (plan assumes 1–2 months with real-world friction).

**LTV:CAC**  
Spend **$30** CAC, get **~$900** LTV → ratio **30:1**.

**Retention math**  
Start year with 200 B2B, **90% retention** → keep 180, so you must add **new** logos on top of that to end at a higher number.

**Pre/post money**  
Company valued at **$3M** before a **$1M** check → **$4M** after; investor owns about **25%** ($1M / $4M).
