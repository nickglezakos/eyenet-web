Who uses EyeNet

Who pays — and what changes

Each persona hits a hard wall with incumbents. EyeNet turns the same cameras into the job they actually need.

HR / facilities lead

The problem now: Never trusts badge sheets or remote presence.

With EyeNet: cameras check people in and push grants to the ERP/CRM (e.g. HubSpot).

What makes them buy EyeNet now: Each staff member uses their own PC or mobile camera to issue presence grants — and complements gate systems. Zero hardware cost; EyeNet scales to companies with thousands of employees.

Shop / mall owner

The problem now: Never sees digital-signage impact with today’s locked CMS.

With EyeNet: who is watching switches the playlist and proves impressions.

What makes them buy EyeNet now: Works on cameras already on their screens, or a cheap off-the-shelf add — no locked player kit, no per-screen SaaS tax. Adaptive playlists and measurable impressions start without a proprietary estate.

Shop manager / store ops

The problem now: Knows someone walked in — not how shoppers move through the floor.

With EyeNet: people counts, paths, heatmaps, dwell, and occupancy with behavioural alerts when zones get busy or quiet.

What makes them buy EyeNet now: Run on cameras already in the store — digests and alerts for managers, video stays local, no separate analytics stack or cloud footage.

Parent / household

The problem now: Pays for cloud cams that ship video off-site when the house is empty.

With EyeNet: a door cam becomes an intelligent doorbell — known and unknown person alerts, plus webhooks to smart-home screens and sound — with footage kept local.

What makes them buy EyeNet now: Plug into cams already at home, or pick up a low-cost everyday camera — no vendor lock-in, no cloud subscription. Default is alerts only; a live view is available only when everyone involved consents.

Student living away

The problem now: Parents want peace of mind for a remote flat / cottage — without a cloud VMS or a security team.

With EyeNet: presence and unknown-person alerts at the remote home; stakeholders get the phone ping without shipping video off-site, and can open a live stream only if all parties agree.

What makes them buy EyeNet now: One cam already at the remote flat or cottage, or a simple budget camera on a shelf — no guard contract, no cloud VMS. Parents get peace of mind without buying a security system or being glued to a live feed.

Care provider / clinician

The problem now: Gets vitals-only RPM with no vision safety layer.

With EyeNet: fall and help-call events enter the health stack without cloud video, with alerts to caregivers and next of kin escalated per settings.

What makes them buy EyeNet now: Use the room cams already at the patient, or add an affordable commodity camera. Next of kin stay assured without being glued to a camera — instant alerts, full awareness of who is on premises and whether caregivers are there and on time, with a live feed available at any time.

Security ops lead

The problem now: Buys storage and per-camera seats, not coverage.

With EyeNet: night posts get vigilance, perimeter, and forensics without 1:1 headcount.

What makes them buy EyeNet now: Cameras already on the fence and floor — vigilance and perimeter without per-camera seats or hiring another night shift.

Captain / plant ops

The problem now: Loses truth when the link drops.

With EyeNet: who’s onboard and who’s in zone stays local; essential updates still reach HQ even on a weak or limited connection. HQs get real-time alerts per settings plus full insights and analytics.

What makes them buy EyeNet now: Run on the ship or plant cams they have, or drop in low-cost ordinary units where coverage is thin — scalable from one site to entire ships, fleets, or industrial areas, with recordings and live feed available; no wait for cloud.
